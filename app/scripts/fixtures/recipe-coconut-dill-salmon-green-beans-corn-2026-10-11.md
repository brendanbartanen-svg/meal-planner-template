## Ingredients
<!-- [C] Costco, [K] Kroger, [P] pantry -->
- 1½ lb salmon fillet in one piece, skin on or off, **fresh** [C — Costco fresh Atlantic side; Kroger seafood counter is the fallback; never frozen]
- 2 tbsp + 1 tsp olive oil [P]
- Kosher salt and black pepper [P]
- **Coconut-dill dressing:** 1 cup unsweetened coconut cream [K — one 13.5-oz can; *not* sweetened "cream of coconut"] or full-fat coconut milk [P] · 2 tbsp dark brown sugar [K — not a pantry staple yet; honey [P] works at 1½ tbsp] · 1 tbsp sherry vinegar [K — or red wine vinegar [P]] · 1 tsp Dijon mustard [P] · zest of 1 lemon + 2 tbsp juice [P] · 1 serrano, finely minced [K] · ½ cup chopped fresh dill [K] · 1 tsp kosher salt · ½ tsp black pepper
- 12 oz green beans, trimmed and halved crosswise [C — 2-lb bag of French beans; the rest roasts as a side later in the week]
- 1½ cups fresh corn kernels, from 2 ears [K — in season; out of season use thawed frozen kernels]
- 1 pint cherry or grape tomatoes, halved or lightly crushed [C — 2-lb clamshell; the remainder is lunch fodder]
- ½ cup more chopped fresh dill to finish [K — 1 cup chopped total is 2 big bunches or 3 clamshells]
- Lemon wedges, from the zested lemon plus one more [P]
- Heavy-duty foil [P]

## Steps
1. Oven to 450°F. Pat the salmon dry and set it on a large sheet of heavy-duty foil (or two stacked regular sheets) on a sheet pan. Rub with 1 tbsp of the oil and salt it directly on both sides, a good ½ tsp total — the fish has to taste seasoned on its own, not just under the dressing.
2. Dressing: whisk the coconut cream, brown sugar, vinegar, mustard, 1 tbsp oil, 1 tsp salt and ½ tsp pepper. Zest the lemon straight into the bowl, squeeze in 2 tbsp juice, then stir in the serrano and ½ cup dill. Taste it — it should read bright, salty and a little sweet, with the chile showing up at the end.
3. Pour half the dressing over the fish and reserve the rest. Fold the foil up and crimp it into a tight packet, adding a second sheet if that's what it takes to seal.
4. On another sheet of foil, toss the green beans with the last 1 tsp oil and a real pinch each of salt and pepper; wrap into a second packet. Both packets on the sheet pan, into the oven 13–15 min. Check the fish through a slit in the top: a knife should slide in with no resistance, 125–130°F at the thickest point. A thick center-cut piece can want 2–3 min more; reseal and return it.
5. Open the bean packet into a large bowl. Add the raw corn, tomatoes, the remaining ½ cup dill, half of the reserved dressing and a pinch of salt; toss. Taste for salt and lemon.
6. Move the fish to a platter, open the packet, and spoon its juices back over the top. Pile on some of the vegetables and the last of the dressing; the rest of the salad and the lemon wedges go alongside. Serve warm, or refrigerate and serve cold.

## Notes
<!-- Cook-to-cook learnings -->
- Added 2026-10-10 from the cook's NYT Cooking PDF. It's a fish night, so it belongs on Sat or Sun with fresh-case salmon per the sourcing rule.
- **What changed from the original:** scaled from a 2-lb fillet (6–8 servings) to 1½ lb for the house 4, with the dressing kept at full quantity so the plate is properly sauced; green beans 8 → 12 oz and corn 1 → 1½ cups, because the salad is the only side; a minced serrano added to the dressing for the house heat level; the fish salted directly. Ratios and method are otherwise as written.
- **Oven vs. grill:** the original cooks the packets on a hot grill, ~10 min covered, with the oven as the stated alternative. The fish steams inside sealed foil either way, so charcoal adds no flavor here; the oven is the default and the kamado is the option when it's already lit (450–500°F direct, dome closed, ~10 min). On the Joe Jr the two packets won't fit side by side — run the salmon on the grate and the beans in the oven, or cut the fish into two packets.
- **Bigger side of salmon:** the dressing as written covers up to 2 lb of fish. If Costco's smallest fresh side runs 2–2½ lb, cook all of it (a 2-lb piece takes 15–18 min) rather than freezing the surplus; it's built to be eaten cold.
- **Scaled to a 3½-lb fillet (~9 servings; W42, 2026-10-10, the cook's ask):** salmon 3½ lb, rubbed with 2 tbsp oil and ~1¼ tsp salt. **Dressing ×2:** 2 cups coconut cream, 3 tbsp honey (or ¼ cup brown sugar), 2 tbsp vinegar, 2 tsp Dijon, 2 tbsp oil, 2 tsp salt, 1 tsp pepper, zest of 2 lemons + ¼ cup juice, 2 serranos, 1 cup dill. **Salad ×2⅓:** 1¾ lb green beans with 2 tsp oil, 3½ cups corn (5 ears), ~2⅓ pints tomatoes, ~1¼ cups dill to finish; wedges from 2 more lemons. Method: cut the fish into two pieces and make two fish packets and two bean packets across two sheet pans (rotate the pans halfway); half the dressing goes over the fish, split between the packets. Time goes by thickness, not weight: 15–18 min for a thick side, same 125–130°F check. Toss the salad in the biggest bowl in the house.
- **Flavor lane:** coconut-lemon-dill — creamy, sweet-tart and herbal. It shares the coconut base with the [coconut fish & tomato bake](coconut-fish-tomato-bake.md) (coconut-lime-turmeric), so don't run the two in back-to-back weeks.
- **No starch on the plate as written** — the corn carries it. Jasmine rice or crusty bread is the add-on if dinner needs something to catch the dressing; neither is in the nutrition estimate.
- **Coconut milk instead of cream:** thinner dressing, ~85 kcal and ~9 g fat less per serving. Use the thick top of an unshaken can if there is one.
- **Corn season:** fresh ears are the point of the salad (they go in raw). From late fall through spring, thawed frozen kernels work; char them in a dry skillet for 2–3 min first so they bring something.
- **Lunch:** eat the leftovers cold, as the original intends — no reheating the fish. Keep the leftover salad and dressing separate from the fish if possible.
