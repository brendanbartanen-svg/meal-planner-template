## Ingredients
<!-- [1] primary store, [2] secondary store, [P] pantry -->

**Filling**
- 1¼ lb lean ground beef (~90/10) [1]
- 1 lb cremini mushrooms, finely chopped [2]
- 1 yellow onion, diced [P]
- 2 carrots, small dice [2]
- 4 cloves garlic, minced [P]
- 4 anchovy fillets, minced (they dissolve — this is the umami spine, don't skip) [2]
- 2 tbsp tomato paste [P]
- 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce [P]
- 1 tsp dried thyme [P]
- 2 tbsp flour [P]
- 1½ cups beef stock (or better-than-bouillon) [P]
- 1 cup frozen peas [2]
- 1 tbsp olive oil [P]
- Salt, black pepper

**Mash**
- 2 lb Yukon gold potatoes [2]
- 4 tbsp butter [P]
- ⅓ cup whole milk, warmed [2]

## Prep
- **Mash:** peel and chunk the potatoes into cold, well-salted water and bring to a simmer — they cook while you prep the rest.
- **Filling:** finely chop the mushrooms; dice the onion; small-dice the carrots; mince the garlic and the anchovies.
- Measure the flour and thyme; stir the Worcestershire into the stock; peas out of the freezer.
- Set an oven rack in the upper third for the broil.

## Steps
1. **Mash first:** simmer the potatoes until fully tender, ~15 min. Mash with the butter and warm milk; season well. Keep it loose enough to spread — tight mash tears the filling.
2. Brown the beef **hard** in a wide broiler-safe skillet — press flat, leave it alone until a deep fond forms, then break up and finish. Salt it directly. Remove, leaving the fat.
3. Mushrooms into the same skillet over high heat with the olive oil; cook until their water is gone and the edges brown, ~8 min (salt them late or they steam). Add the onion and carrot, 5 min more.
4. Garlic, anchovies, tomato paste, and thyme; cook until the paste darkens, ~2 min. Beef back in, dust with the flour, 1 min. Stock + Worcestershire, scraping up the fond; simmer to a thick, glossy gravy, ~5 min. Stir in the peas off heat. **Season aggressively** — the mash mutes everything above it.
5. Spread the mash over the filling and drag a fork through it for peaks. Broil until the peaks are deep golden, 3–5 min — watch it, don't walk away. Rest 10 min so the gravy sets from soup to sauce.
