## Ingredients
<!-- [1] primary store, [2] secondary store, [P] pantry -->
- 1½ lb salmon fillet in one piece, skin on or off, **fresh** [1 — the warehouse club fresh Atlantic side; the supermarket seafood counter is the fallback; never frozen]
- 2 tbsp + 1 tsp olive oil [P]
- Kosher salt and black pepper [P]
- **Coconut-dill dressing:** 1 cup unsweetened coconut cream [2 — one 13.5-oz can; *not* sweetened "cream of coconut"] or full-fat coconut milk [P] · 2 tbsp dark brown sugar [2 — not a pantry staple yet; honey [P] works at 1½ tbsp] · 1 tbsp sherry vinegar [2 — or red wine vinegar [P]] · 1 tsp Dijon mustard [P] · zest of 1 lemon + 2 tbsp juice [P] · 1 serrano, finely minced [2] · ½ cup chopped fresh dill [2] · 1 tsp kosher salt · ½ tsp black pepper
- 12 oz green beans, trimmed and halved crosswise [1 — 2-lb bag of French beans; the rest roasts as a side later in the week]
- 1½ cups fresh corn kernels, from 2 ears [2 — in season; out of season use thawed frozen kernels]
- 1 pint cherry or grape tomatoes, halved or lightly crushed [1 — 2-lb clamshell; the remainder is lunch fodder]
- ½ cup more chopped fresh dill to finish [2 — 1 cup chopped total is 2 big bunches or 3 clamshells]
- Lemon wedges, from the zested lemon plus 1 more [P]
- Heavy-duty foil [P]

## Prep
- Oven to 450°F. Tear two large sheets of heavy-duty foil (or two doubled regular sheets), one for the fish and one for the beans; the fish sheet goes on a sheet pan.
- **Salmon:** pat dry and set it on the foil. Rub with 1 tbsp of the oil and salt it directly on both sides, a good ½ tsp total — the fish has to taste seasoned on its own, not just under the dressing.
- **Coconut-dill dressing:** mince the serrano; chop all the dill, dressing and finish. Whisk the coconut cream, brown sugar, vinegar, mustard, 1 tbsp oil, and the dressing's salt and pepper. Zest the lemon straight into the bowl, squeeze in the juice, then stir in the serrano and the dressing dill. Taste it — it should read bright, salty and a little sweet, with the chile showing up at the end.
- **Salad:** trim and halve the green beans, cut the corn off the cobs, halve or lightly crush the tomatoes.
- Cut the lemon wedges.

## Steps
1. Pour half the dressing over the fish and reserve the rest. Fold the foil up and crimp it into a tight packet, adding a second sheet if that's what it takes to seal.
2. On the second sheet of foil, toss the green beans with the last 1 tsp oil and a real pinch each of salt and pepper; wrap into a second packet. Both packets on the sheet pan, into the oven 13–15 min. Check the fish through a slit in the top: a knife should slide in with no resistance, 125–130°F at the thickest point. A thick center-cut piece can want 2–3 min more; reseal and return it.
3. Open the bean packet into a large bowl. Add the raw corn, tomatoes, the finishing dill, half of the reserved dressing and a pinch of salt; toss. Taste for salt and lemon.
4. Move the fish to a platter, open the packet, and spoon its juices back over the top. Pile on some of the vegetables and the last of the dressing; the rest of the salad and the lemon wedges go alongside. Serve warm, or refrigerate and serve cold.

## Notes
<!-- Cook-to-cook learnings -->
- **What changed from the original:** scaled from a 2-lb fillet (6–8 servings) to 1½ lb for the house 4, with the dressing kept at full quantity so the plate is properly sauced; green beans 8 → 12 oz and corn 1 → 1½ cups, because the salad is the only side; a minced serrano added to the dressing for the house heat level; the fish salted directly. Ratios and method are otherwise as written.
- **Oven vs. grill:** the original cooks the packets on a hot grill, ~10 min covered, with the oven as the stated alternative. The fish steams inside sealed foil either way, so charcoal adds no flavor here; the oven is the default and the kamado is the option when it's already lit (450–500°F direct, dome closed, ~10 min). On the Joe Jr the two packets won't fit side by side — run the salmon on the grate and the beans in the oven, or cut the fish into two packets.
- **Bigger side of salmon:** the dressing as written covers up to 2 lb of fish. If the warehouse club's smallest fresh side runs 2–2½ lb, cook all of it (a 2-lb piece takes 15–18 min) rather than freezing the surplus; it's built to be eaten cold.
- **No starch on the plate as written** — the corn carries it. Jasmine rice or crusty bread is the add-on if dinner needs something to catch the dressing; neither is in the nutrition estimate.
- **Coconut milk instead of cream:** thinner dressing, ~85 kcal and ~9 g fat less per serving. Use the thick top of an unshaken can if there is one.
- **Corn season:** fresh ears are the point of the salad (they go in raw). From late fall through spring, thawed frozen kernels work; char them in a dry skillet for 2–3 min first so they bring something.
- **Lunch:** eat the leftovers cold, as the original intends — no reheating the fish. Keep the leftover salad and dressing separate from the fish if possible.
