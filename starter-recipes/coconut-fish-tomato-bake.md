## Ingredients
<!-- [1] primary store, [2] secondary store, [P] pantry -->
- 4 fish fillets, ~6 oz each, **fresh**, skin on or off — whatever looks best at the fresh case: snapper, haddock, cod, halibut, or salmon [1/2 — the warehouse club fresh counter if stocked (cod, halibut, salmon are the usual), else the supermarket seafood counter; never frozen]
- **Marinade:** ¾ cup full-fat coconut milk [P — about half a can; see Notes for the rest] · 1 tbsp grated ginger [P] · 3 cloves garlic, grated [P] · ½ tsp turmeric [P] · 1 tsp red pepper flakes [P] · 1 tbsp honey [P] · 1 tsp kosher salt [P] · zest + juice of 1 lime [P] · ¼ cup chopped cilantro [2]
- 2 pints cherry or grape tomatoes [1 — ~1¼ lb; 2-lb clamshell; the remainder is lunch fodder]
- 3 tbsp olive oil [P]
- 1 lime, in wedges [P]
- ¼ cup more chopped cilantro to finish [2 — 1 bunch covers both draws]
- 1 serrano, thinly sliced [2] — optional, for the medium-hot version
- 1¼ cups jasmine rice [P]
- Kosher salt [P]

## Prep
- Rice on.
- **Fish:** salt the fillets directly on both sides (a good ½ tsp total) and let them sit while you build the marinade — season the component, not just the sauce around it.
- **Marinade:** grate the ginger and garlic; chop the cilantro, both draws. Whisk the coconut milk, ginger, garlic, turmeric, pepper flakes, honey, and the marinade salt; zest and juice the lime straight into the bowl; stir in the marinade cilantro. Turn the fish in it and refrigerate 15–30 min, no longer.
- Oven to 425°F, one rack lower-middle and one at the top for the broiler.
- **Finish:** slice the serrano if using; cut the lime into wedges.

## Steps
1. Tomatoes on a half-sheet pan (smaller and they steam), 2 tbsp of the oil, a real pinch of salt, toss. Nestle the fish between the tomatoes, spoon every bit of marinade over the fillets, drizzle the last tbsp of oil over the fish. Lower-middle rack, 8–10 min by thickness: surface opaque, center still translucent — it should not flake yet.
2. Pull the pan and switch the broiler to high. Top rack, 5–6 min, rotating once, until the fish is just tender and the tomatoes are blistered and browning in spots. The marinade and tomato juices tighten into a silky pan sauce as it broils. Thin fillets (fluke, thin snapper) can be done in 3–4 min; salmon wants the full time, to 125–130°F.
3. Taste the pan sauce for salt while it's still in the pan. Plate the fish and tomatoes over rice and tip all the juices over the top — the sauce is the whole point. Finish with the remaining cilantro, the serrano if using, and lime wedges for squeezing.

## Notes
<!-- Cook-to-cook learnings -->
- **What changed from the original:** garlic 1 → 3 cloves, red pepper flakes ½ → 1 tsp plus the optional serrano, the fish salted directly before marinating, and jasmine rice added as the plate (the original suggests toasted baguette or steamed rice). Method, ratios, and timings are otherwise as written.
- **Flavor lane:** coconut-lime-turmeric — a third fish lane next to tomato-Mediterranean (puttanesca, crazy water) and Asian-sesame (ahi bowls). The tomatoes are in it, so don't run it the week right after a puttanesca-style fish; the coconut and lime carry the profile, not olives and capers.
- **Coconut milk:** ¾ cup is about half a can. The rest keeps 4–5 days refrigerated (or freezes in an ice-cube tray) — plan a curry the same week or stir it into a pot of rice.
- **Salmon version:** ~100 kcal and ~10 g fat more per serving than the white-fish estimate above.
- **Lunch reheat:** fish at 50% power or eaten warm; the sauce and rice reheat fully.
